## In one paragraph

June 2767, the high water on the upper Rio Negro, by the rapids of São Gabriel. Dr. Viktor Lindqvist of the Royal
Academy keeps a research station on the bank, and in a crate in its specimen house he keeps the only one of its kind
any man has seen: a hatchling from a leathery egg that a river trader found after a flood came down from the mountains
in the north. He calls her LINNEA. She grows faster than anything he knows; he means to make his name with her, and to
breed her. The Crown's mapmaker Duarte Valadares comes down the river from the boundary survey and finds the tracks
round the station far too big for her. On the Eve of Saint John, while the mission village lights its bonfires,
Lindqvist sets out his scent-lure to catch her a mate, and her kin answer: a wild pack out of the flooded forest, and
behind it a Tyrant Sire. The station holds the night. At first light the pack goes back into the forest and the Sire
lies dead by her pen. Lindqvist has won: she is his. That victory is the worst thing that could have happened.

  breeds the creatures in secret; they get loose and the Brood is born around a Queen"). The prologue never says what

## The central thing: SPECIMEN ONE (Linnea)

WHAT LINDQVIST THINKS SHE IS: an unknown order of reptile, the find of the century, a hatchling of some lost lineage
that came down the river in a flood. Raised by hand from the egg, she eats from his hand and follows his whistle. He
weighs her every morning (the hub's line "He writes down its weight every morning. It grows." is his habit from her).
He means to breed her (a pair, then a line), to give the Academy a new creature for the colony's work, and to put his
own name on a new order of creation.

WHAT SHE IS (the King knows; the player only guesses, question 5): a young BROOD MOTHER. Signs a Brood player can
read, never said: a little frill like the Mother's; she does not hunt, she CALLS; and in Part 3 the Tyrant Sire does
not try to eat her: he ROOTS beside her pen, as a Sire roots beside a Mother to father her eggs.

PRIDE: Lindqvist's scent-lure. From the shell of her egg and her own musk he has made an ATTRACTANT, to bring a male
of her kind to the station and pen him beside her. Valadares: a man may study what God made; he may not set himself to
make it over. (The owner words it.)

DECEPTION (tone rule: the Brood side's narrators may be mistaken or deceived; the Crown's never are): Lindqvist writes
that his lure brought the Sire, and that his pens and his whistle held the night. He hides from Lieutenant Teles how
big the tracks are (choice 1), and if he keeps her he reports to the Academy and the Crown that specimen one "died of a
fever". The King's closing lines say the plain truth: the lure brought nothing; SHE called them. And she did not die.

### How the pack comes (the calling rule)

1. THE CALL (scripted, heard): Linnea calls on a timetable (a sound, a ping on her pen); each call is answered from the
   forest (a roar, a ping at a trail head) and a wave spawns at that trail head a few seconds later. The waves go for
   her pen first (attack-move to it), then for whatever is near. So the player learns where they come from and why.
2. THE LURE (the narrator's pride): Lindqvist's three lure jars, set at the forest edge in Part 2, draw the FIRST
   waves to themselves (an ambush the player can prepare). From then on the creatures ignore the jars: they go for her.
   Fair, because the game says it once it happens ("They walked past the jars."); and it is the narrator's own
   mistake, played.
3. THE SWAMP (the Brood's own rule): the rooted Mound Turtle's swamp heals the Brood standing on it and lets the Mist
   Toad lay fog. Killing the turtle is the player's lever.
4. THE CEILING: waves are timed and capped per part (never more than N creatures alive), so the night is a rising
   pressure that ends at first light, never a grind.

## The choices

### 1. Tell the lieutenant (end of Part 1)

LINDQVIST: "If Teles sees those tracks he will shoot everything in this forest, beginning with her. One night. I

- KEEP IT QUIET (Lindqvist): the station alone at nightfall; the village must be brought in under attack (fewer saved);

- Canon (proposed): KEEP IT QUIET. It is the narrator's sin, the first of the scientists' deceptions, and why the Crown
  later distrusts the Academy.

### 2. At the pen: keep her or put her down (at first light, only if she was not lost)

The Sire lies dead by the broken pen; Linnea calls once more, and nothing answers.
LINDQVIST: "She is the only one. Do you understand? If she dies, everything I have done tonight was for nothing."
VALADARES: "Everything you did tonight was for her, Doctor. That is what I am afraid of. Let me do it. Quickly."

- KEEP HER (Lindqvist): she goes into a stronger crate and down into the station's cellar; Lindqvist writes to the
  Academy (and, if Teles was told, to Teles) that specimen one "died of a fever" (flag linnea = kept). The way to the
  Brood of Act 2: she is the Queen it is born around.
- PUT HER DOWN (Valadares): he does it himself, with a prayer for the creature; Lindqvist keeps her skin and her
  bones (the jar in the hub) and goes north for eggs to start again (flag linnea = killed). Act 2's Queen comes from
  the Specimens' eggs instead.
- (No choice) LOST: if the Sire took her (Part 3), she is gone into the forest (flag linnea = lost): Valadares follows
  their tracks north at once (the Lost Plateau, where she is among her own).
- Canon (proposed): KEEP HER. The King remembers it.

## How a WON mission stays honest (and fun)

- It is a real win: hold the night, kill the Sire, keep the people. Every objective is winnable; skill shows in the
  count of the saved, the turtle killed early, Pieter alive, the gun kept.
- The only real defeat is Lindqvist's or Valadares' death (retry).
- The victory is NOT cheapened: the end screen says the station held (a title from the ending, the Clockwork list's
  story ending: "THE STATION HOLDS" over still b06), then the tally, then the King's two lines that turn it.
- The waves are timed and capped and the dawn countdown is on screen from the start of Part 3: no endless night.
- The night's mechanics are the Brood's own (the call, a Sire rooting by a female, the turtle's swamp, rooted
  creatures not biting): the player learns how the Brood works by fighting it, and Lost Plateau's mirror then lets him
  use what he learned.
- The lure is the narrator's mistake played, never a trick on the player: it works once, then the game says plainly
  that it no longer does.

## Co-op and versus

- CO-OP: the second player commands the GARRISON (purple: Teles' Musketeers, the gun, Frei Joaquim and the villagers'
  escort). The station builds and holds the pens; the garrison holds the causeway and walks the village in. With
  choice 1 = quiet, the second player starts with fewer men and arrives later (the story's price, said in the
  briefing). The choices are the host's.
- VERSUS: the second player IS THE PACK (green), the first versus in which a player commands Brood creatures. He gets
  the waves by Linnea's calls (each call gives him a group at a trail head of his choice) and, in Part 3, the Tyrant
  Sire; his choices are the Brood's own: where to root the Mound Turtle, when to root the Sire by the pen. He wins by
  breaking the pen and walking Linnea out by a north trail (she follows the Sire when freed), or by killing Lindqvist.
  The station wins by holding to first light with her in the pen.
- SOLO MIRROR "THE CALLING" (question 1): the versus with the station run by the script, opened after the prologue.
