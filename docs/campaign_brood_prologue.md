## In one paragraph

June 2767, the high water on the upper Rio Negro, by the rapids of São Gabriel. Dr. Viktor Lindqvist of the Royal
Academy keeps a research station on the bank, and in a crate in its specimen house he keeps the only one of its kind
any man has seen: a hatchling from a leathery egg that a river trader found after a flood came down from the mountains
in the north. He calls her LINNEA. He means to tame her, breed her and sell her line to the Crown as BEASTS OF WAR.
The Crown's mapmaker Duarte Valadares comes down the river from the boundary survey, finds the tracks round the station
far too big for her, and warns him; he is not listened to. On the Eve of Saint John, while the mission village lights
its bonfires, Lindqvist sets out his scent-lure to catch her a mate, and her kin answer: a wild pack out of the flooded
forest, and behind it a Tyrant Sire. The station does not hold. The Sire roots beside her pen, she lays a clutch of
eggs in the straw, and before first light he breaks the pen and leads her into the forest. The station burns. In the
burning specimen house the player chooses: burn the eggs, or carry them out with the notes. The survivors leave by the
jetty. The scientists have lost what they thought they owned; Lindqvist tells it as if it had been stolen from him.

## Where it sits in the story

- Year: JUNE 2767 (the Eve of Saint John, 23 June), a few weeks before Virginia (summer 2767) and six months before
  Nuremberg (Advent 2767). All three prologues happen in the same year: three first fires, the Brood's the earliest.
  The play order stays as the campaign has it (the Crown's Prologue first; the flashbacks may go back in time).
- The King's frame: in the Crown's hub Tomasz already says "Two letters came for the Governor that week: one from an
  explorer in the Amazon, one from a clockmaker." The explorer's letter is VALADARES' REPORT OF THIS NIGHT, sent to the
  Crown's governors (one copy reached Fairleigh in Virginia). The King opens and closes the mission with that letter in
  front of him; LINDQVIST TELLS IT HIMSELF in between, in the present (owner answer 2), and the King corrects him from
  the letter.
- Three prologues, three losses of a different kind: the Crown's is a harsh win (Virginia), the Clockwork's makers
  lose a CITY (Nuremberg), the Brood's scientists lose CONTROL of what they thought they owned (one creature, a
  station, and, if he saves them, a clutch of eggs that will be the Brood). The Clockwork faction is born that night;
  the Brood is born of this night years later (Act 2: "Lindqvist breeds the creatures in secret; they get loose and
  the Brood is born around a Queen"). The prologue never says what Linnea is.

## The central thing: SPECIMEN ONE (Linnea)

WHAT LINDQVIST THINKS SHE IS: an unknown order of reptile, the find of the century, a hatchling of some lost lineage
that came down the river in a flood. Raised by hand from the egg, she eats from his hand and follows his whistle. He
weighs her every morning (the hub's line "He writes down its weight every morning. It grows." is his habit from her).
HIS STATED AIM (owner answer 7): to tame her, breed her, and sell her line to the Crown as BEASTS OF WAR, "a cavalry
that needs no hay and fears no cannon", and to put his own name on a new order of creation. That aim is also why he
hides the tracks from Lieutenant Teles: he means to show the Crown a finished, tame beast, never a danger.

WHAT SHE IS (the King knows; the player only guesses): a young BROOD MOTHER (owner answer 9: the Queen is never seen,
only hinted). Signs a Brood player can read, never said: a little frill like the Mother's; she does not hunt, she
CALLS; in Part 3 the Tyrant Sire does not try to eat her, he ROOTS beside her pen, as a Sire roots beside a Mother,
and while he is rooted she lays a clutch of eggs in her straw (the Brood's own rule, played once, never explained).

PRIDE: Lindqvist's scent-lure. From the shell of her egg and her own musk he has made an ATTRACTANT, to bring a male
of her kind to the station and pen him beside her. Valadares: a man may study what God made; he may not set himself to
make it over, still less into a weapon. (The owner words it.)

DECEPTION (tone rule: the Brood side's narrators may be mistaken or deceived; the Crown's never are): Lindqvist says
his lure brought the Sire, that she was stolen, that she would have stayed with him, and (if he carried them out) that
he saved "the research". The King's closing lines say the plain truth from Valadares' letter: the lure brought
nothing, SHE called them; she was never his; she went home; and what he carried out of the fire were her children.

### How the pack comes (the calling rule)

1. THE CALL (scripted, heard): Linnea calls on a timetable (a sound, a ping on her pen); each call is answered from the
   forest (a roar, a ping at a trail head) and a wave spawns at that trail head a few seconds later. The waves go for
   her pen first (attack-move to it), then for whatever is near. So the player learns where they come from and why.
2. THE LURE (the narrator's pride): Lindqvist's three lure jars, set at the forest edge in Part 2, draw the FIRST
   waves to themselves (an ambush the player can prepare). From then on the creatures ignore the jars: they go for her.
   Fair, because the game says it once it happens ("They walked past the jars."); and it is the narrator's own
   mistake, played (open question 2).
3. THE SWAMP (the Brood's own rule): the rooted Mound Turtle's swamp heals the Brood standing on it and lets the Mist
   Toad lay fog. Killing the turtle is the player's lever, and in Part 3 it buys time (see the fall).
4. THE CEILING: waves are timed and capped per part (never more than N creatures alive): a rising pressure that ends
   at the time shown, never a grind.

box), Raptors before him, Spitters on the swamp. He walks through the palisade as if it were reeds and ROOTS beside her

The countdown is on screen from that moment: "Benedito's canoes leave in 8:40".

LINDQVIST (as the Sire roots): "He did not want to eat her. He lay down beside her like a groom."

- THE CLUTCH: while the Sire is rooted, eggs appear in her straw, one every half minute (4-6).

## The choices (two, owner answer 8)

### 1. Tell the lieutenant (end of Part 1)

VALADARES: "Send to Teles, Doctor. Tell him what we saw at the creek, and bring the village in before dark."
LINDQVIST: "If Teles sees those tracks he will shoot everything in this forest, beginning with her. The Crown will
have its beasts tame or not at all. One night."

- TELL HIM (Valadares): the village is safe at the fort before dark (many can be saved); the garrison holds the
  causeway and the gun is at the station; in Part 3 the fort's steps are a second landing for the garrison's people.
  The Crown knows what the station kept (flag teles = told).
- KEEP IT QUIET (Lindqvist): the station alone at nightfall; the village must be brought in under attack (fewer
  saved); Teles arrives late; the Crown does not know what the station kept (flag teles = quiet).
- Canon (proposed): KEEP IT QUIET. It is the narrator's first deception, and with choice 2 = save it is how the eggs
  leave the Amazon unknown to the Crown.

### 2. In the burning specimen house: burn the eggs or save the research (Part 3)

The roof is alight. The eggs lie in the trampled straw; his notes and the specimen book are on the bench.
VALADARES: "Burn them, Doctor. Whatever she was, do not carry her children down this river."
LINDQVIST: "Ten years of work. The Crown's beasts are in that straw. I will not leave them to the fire."

- BURN THE EGGS (Valadares): he throws the lamp into the straw; the notes burn with them; Lindqvist walks to the jetty
  free and armed (flag clutch = burned). He leaves with nothing but his word: the Specimens is his whole second chance.
- SAVE THE RESEARCH (Lindqvist): he carries a crate with the eggs and the notes himself (a carried object: he walks
  slower and cannot shoot until he is aboard), so the player saves fewer people on the way: the price is played, not
  told (flag clutch = saved). The eggs hatch downriver in secret; the way to Act 2's breeding and to the Specimens.
- Canon (proposed): SAVE THE RESEARCH. The King remembers it.
- Linnea is ALWAYS lost to the forest (no flag needed; the canon).

## How a lost mission stays fair and fun (the Clockwork's section as the model)

- The player is never told "you lose" for something he did not do. The only real defeat is Lindqvist's or Valadares'
  death (retry).
- The story's loss is announced when it comes, not before: Parts 1-2 are played to win (the pen built, the goats, the
  lure ambush, the village, the turtle: real small wins); at Part 3's start the objectives change on screen and the
  countdown is shown, so nobody grinds at a fight he cannot win.
- Skill shows: every villager walked in, the turtle killed early, the Sire driven up, Pieter alive, the gun kept move a
  number the player can see, and it carries forward. A perfect run still loses the station and Linnea, and saves
  nearly everyone.
- The night's mechanics are the Brood's own (the call, a Sire rooting by a female, her eggs while he roots, the
  turtle's swamp, rooted creatures not biting): the player learns how the Brood works by losing to it, and the Lost
  Plateau's mirror then lets him use what he learned.
- The lure is the narrator's mistake played, never a trick on the player: it works once, then the game says plainly
  that it no longer does. The Sire that cannot die is said plainly too.
- No grind: waves are timed and capped; the canoes leave on time.
- The end screen is not "DEFEAT": it says THE STATION IS LOST over the ending's still, then the tally (saved, the
  clutch, Pieter, the gun, the choices) and where the story goes. (The Clockwork list's story ending.)
