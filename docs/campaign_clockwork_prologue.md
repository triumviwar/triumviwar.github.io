# The Clockwork Prologue: The Thirteenth Hour (Nuremberg, 2767) — OUTLINE

PROPOSAL by Claude (job clockwork-prologue-outline, 2026-11-15) for the owner to change freely. It follows the shape of
the Crown's Prologue (docs/campaign_mission1.md). Plot, play, choices and stills are proposals. EVERY LINE OF DIALOGUE
IS A PLACEHOLDER for the owner to rewrite; they are kept few and plain on purpose. Names marked (proposed) come from
docs/campaign_timeline.md or from this doc.

## The owner's idea (2026-11-15)

Every faction gets a prologue. The Clockwork's: "we play as the good Clockwork, on the side of the inventor, attempting
to do something; all of a sudden we discover this failing mechanism that keeps replicating itself, but we discover it
too late. It's already spreading; we try to fight it down, to contain it, but we lose. A losing mission: we lose, and
the Clockwork is able to expand."

His answers (21:34): the self-replicating machine IS the whole Clockwork faction (the original Clockwork was the
inventor's; he loses control of it); the inventor is MEISTER HOLLWEG (his Nuremberg workshop is already the Clockwork
hub: docs/campaign_hub_art.md); he was trying to build THE PERFECT MACHINE.

## In one paragraph

Advent, 2767. In Nuremberg, Meister Hollweg of the Guild of the Escapement wakes his life's work: THE PERPETUAL, a
clockwork that winds itself, mends itself and builds more of itself, so that no clock need ever stop and no man need
ever labour. For one day it is a marvel. By dusk the guild finds a second gear network grown in the river mills that
nobody built, and some of the Perpetual's own children have fallen out of step with their master. The guild fights to
stop it, then to contain it, then only to save what it can. At midnight the great clock of the Frauenkirche strikes
thirteen: the machine has the city. Hollweg escapes through the west gate with what the player saved. The Clockwork
is loose in the world.

## Where it sits in the story (see OWNER QUESTION 1)

- Year: ADVENT 2767, about five months after Virginia (summer 2767). Both prologues happen in the same year: the

- The King's frame: in the Crown's hub Tomasz already says "Two letters came for the Governor that week: one from an
  explorer in the Amazon, one from a clockmaker." That first letter (summer) was PROUD: Hollweg offering his machines to
  rebuild Virginia's barns. This prologue is told from his SECOND letter (winter), the one that begs the Crown to burn

- Knock-on for docs/campaign_act1.md (proposal): Act 1's Clockwork braid becomes the guild IN EXILE working for the
  Crown with what it saved (mission 2 "The First Automata": Hollweg builds again, "safe this time"; mission 4

  city of gears in Brazil" can become: the Perpetual's crates shipped to Brazil before the fall wake there (see the

## The Perfect Machine: THE PERPETUAL (name proposed)

WHAT IT WAS MEANT TO BE: the guild's answer to wear. Every clock stops in the end because a man must wind it and a
man must mend it. The Perpetual winds itself from the river (the mills' wheels), mends itself, and, because even a
mended machine wears out, it BUILDS ITS OWN SUCCESSORS from its master pattern before it wears out. Hollweg's dream:
a city kept like the great clock of the Guild ("It has not lost a second in forty years. Hollweg says a city could be
kept the same way": the hub already says it).

IN GAME TERMS it is simply the Clockwork as the player knows it: the Clocktower is "the blueprint of the machine"
(Defs), it raises Winders, every building must mesh with it, its Regulators keep the machines "in step". The prologue
reveals what those rules MEAN in the story.

WHY HE BUILT IT TO MAKE MORE OF ITSELF: pride dressed as charity: "so no man need labour". Ferrer's objection, in
the house tone: a thing that makes its own kind and answers to nobody is a thing that has no master; only God makes
what makes itself (the owner words it).

THE FIRST SIGN IT FAILS: clocks have a STOP-WORK, a small part that stops a spring being wound past its limit. The
Perpetual's pattern was drawn without one, so nothing tells a copy when the work is done. The copies keep copying;
each copy is a little more out of step with the master. The signs, in order: a Cog Press pressing cogs nobody ordered;
Winders walking away from their posts toward the river; a gear network found under the mills, already meshed and
already making more. Found too late.

THE TRUTH THE NARRATOR HIDES (tone rule: the Clockwork's narrators may be mistaken or deceived; the Crown's never are):
Hollweg writes that "a tooth slipped". The King's closing line says the plain truth: the stop-work was not forgotten,
it was left out on purpose, because a machine that can stop is not perpetual. (OWNER QUESTION 4 offers the
alternatives.)

### How the machine grows (the replication rule)

Two parts, both close to what the engine already does:

1. SEEDS (scripted, visible): the machine's Clocktowers are tagged SEEDS (seed_mill, seed_baths, seed_market,
   seed_castle...). On a timetable each living seed spawns machines round it and, at set times, a new seed "unfolds" at
   the next seed area (the `build` action). A seed that is DEAD spawns nothing more: every spawn trigger is
   `{"all": [{"time": t}, {"not": {"dead": "seed_mill"}}]}`. So killing a seed early really slows the spread, and the
   player sees it.
2. THE NETWORK (the game's own rule): the machine's buildings must mesh with a Clocktower like any Clockwork's, and
   its constructs wind down away from their gear ground. Breaking the links at the bridges stops everything beyond
   them, and machines lured off their gears run down. The containment is the faction's real mechanics, not a script.
   (Optionally the machine team is also run by the normal Clockwork computer, `ai:normal:balanced`, with a trickle of
   cogs, so it trains and builds on its own between the scripted beats: to be tried in the build.)

The ceiling: a cap per part (no more than N machines spawn while the player is busy elsewhere), so it is a growing
tide, never an endless grind.

## Part 1: THE PERPETUAL WAKES (day, about 6-8 minutes)

- Start: the workshop's Clocktower (THE PERPETUAL, tagged perpetual, sleeping), 2 Cog Presses, 4 Winders, a Light
  Works half built, 4 Clock Soldiers. Hollweg, Jakob and Ferrer stand in the courtyard. A teaching part for the
  faction: wind the presses, finish the Light Works, assemble a Regulator, train Clock Soldiers.
- Objective: fill the Crown's order: 6 Clock Soldiers to the Crown's wagons at the market (a `reach`), where the Watch
  will take them. (The guild serves the Crown here: it is the "good" Clockwork.)
- THE GIFT (about 4:00): Hollweg wakes the Perpetual. For a minute the Clocktower raises Winders BY ITSELF (free
  spawns on the player's team, tagged children) and they set to work unasked. It should feel good: free workers.
  HOLLWEG: "There. It needs no hand but its own."
  FERRER: "It is beautiful. That is what frightens me."
- Small oddities while the part ends, easy to miss: a child Winder idles facing the river; a Cog Press shows cogs that
  nobody wound.
- Still c04 (the Perpetual wakes) in the dialogue box at the gift.

## Part 2: THE MILL ISLAND (dusk, about 6-8 minutes)

- The light turns to dusk. JAKOB (from the river): "Meister, there is a press turning on the mill island. We never
  built a press there."
- Objective: GO AND LOOK (reach the mill island with Jakob and an escort). A Netter is given: CATCH ONE (net a red
  machine and bring the Watch to it: the player sees with his own eyes that it is a guild machine, in red).
- DISCOVERED TOO LATE (still c05): the island is a whole red network, meshed and already making more; a reveal shows
  the player a second seed in the old bath houses north of the river. 
- THE TURN: some of the Perpetual's children on the player's team fall out of step: they stutter for 5 seconds
  (fair warning: a sound and a mark), then change team to the machine. Only children made by the gift turn, never the
  units the player trained. (Needs the engine's `convert`, below.)
- Objective: DESTROY THE MILL SEED. Real and winnable early; the longer the player waits, the more it has spread.
- THE CHOICE (below): ring the bells or keep it quiet.
- The part ends whatever happens at the mill: at nightfall a THIRD seed unfolds by the market (or at the castle if the
  market's was found), and the spread outruns the guild.

## Part 3: THE THIRTEENTH HOUR (night, about 9-12 minutes)

The objectives change, plainly: "It cannot be stopped tonight. Save what you can." The great clock's hands become the
mission's countdown (the objectives panel: "The city strikes thirteen in 8:40").

- PRIMARY: HOLLWEG MUST LIVE and reach the west gate before the thirteenth stroke. (The ONLY way to lose the mission
  for real: retry. Everything else is the story's loss, not the player's.)
- OPTIONAL, each one carried forward:
  - THE TOWNSFOLK: groups of citizens come out of the houses as the machine reaches their streets; escort them to the
    west gate (they leave the map and are counted: "Saved 34 of 120").
  - CUT THE NETWORK: destroy the machine's links on the bridges; the north half of its network stops, and the fall
    comes later (more time to save people).
  - JAKOB: he lives (he went back for the workshop's log).
  - THE PATTERN: reach the workshop and take Hollweg's master drawings out (a carried object: a cart or Jakob himself),
    or burn them where they lie. See the gate choice.
- THE FALL, never cheap: the clock is shown from the start of the part; the fall comes at the time shown and never
  sooner. Each seed the player has killed and each bridge cut puts it back (written as two or three triggers: at T if
  the machine still has 4 seeds, at T + 90 s if it has 3, at T + 180 s in any case). The machine grows at the pace
  the player can see; the more he cuts, the more he saves.
- At the stroke (still c06 begins): the bells of every church toll thirteen, the Watch falls back, the last citizens
  run. The gate choice is taken (or the default) and the mission ends.
- Difficulty: the fall's time and the seeds' spawn sizes scale (Easy gives two more minutes).

## The choices

### 1. The bells (Part 2, after the mill)

FERRER: "Ring the bells, Meister. Let the city wake and go."
HOLLWEG: "If the city wakes, the guild is finished. Give me one hour and I will put it right."

- RING THE BELLS (Ferrer): the townsfolk start leaving at once (Part 3 starts with more of them already on the road:
  many more can be saved); the Watch joins in force at the bridges; Hollweg is disgraced (flag bells = rung).
- KEEP IT QUIET (Hollweg): one hour: the guild gets cogs and Tension and a Pendulum early, but the citizens wake only
  when the machine is in their streets (far fewer can be saved); the Watch arrives late (flag bells = quiet).
- Canon (proposed): KEEP IT QUIET. It is the narrator's sin and the reason he writes the letter.

### 2. At the gate: the people or the pattern (the ending)

The last cart through the west gate has room for one thing.

- THE PEOPLE: the last citizens ride out; Hollweg's drawings burn in the workshop. Nobody alive knows the Perpetual's
  pattern; it cannot be studied or stopped from inside (flag gate = people).
- THE PATTERN: the drawings ride out; the last citizens do not. The guild can build again from them (Act 1's "First
  Automata" in exile, the temptation; at the Council of Rome they are the evidence) (flag gate = pattern).
- Canon (proposed): THE PATTERN. Ferrer remembers it.

## How a losing mission stays fair and fun

- The player is never told "you lose" for something he did not do. The only real defeat is Hollweg's death.
- The story's loss is announced BEFORE it comes: the objectives say so plainly at Part 3's start, the countdown is on
  screen, the clock strikes at the time shown.
- Skill shows: every seed killed, every bridge cut, every street escorted moves a number the player can see, and it
  carries forward. A perfect run still loses the city and saves nearly everyone.
- The early parts are winnable in their own terms (the order filled, the mill seed destroyed): small wins inside the
  big loss.
- No grind: the machine's growth is timed and capped; the fall ends the mission on time.
- The end screen is not "DEFEAT": it says NUREMBERG IS LOST over the ending's still, then the tally (saved, cut, Jakob,
  the choices) and where the story goes. (Engine list, item 1.)

## Co-op and versus

- CO-OP: the second player commands the WATCH (purple: the Musketeers at the bridges and the castle, and the townsfolk's
  escort). The guild builds and fights; the Watch holds the bridges and walks the people out. Like Ruggieri's guns in
  the Crown's Prologue. The choices are the host's.
- VERSUS: the second player IS THE MACHINE (red). He starts with the mill seed (hidden), grows by building and
  training like a Clockwork player, and wins by making the city strike thirteen early (holding 5 Clocktowers at once)
  or by killing Hollweg. The guild wins the versus by getting Hollweg out with half the townsfolk. A fitting first
  "co-op versus": two Clockwork players, one making, one unmaking.
