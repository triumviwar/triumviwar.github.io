## The rules this plan follows

From CLAUDE.md and the campaign docs. Nothing here bends them.

- The game is Catholic and monarchist. The Crown and the Church are the good side. No twist on that.
- The King tells the story truthfully. Other tellers may be wrong or deceived (Hollweg, Lindqvist). The Crown's tellers
  never are (Tomasz, Valadares, Ferrer, Ruggieri, Fairleigh).
- THE GHOST IN THE MACHINE: the Post-Modern Age fell to its own thinking machines. Common people hate clever machines.
- THE BROOD WAS MADE, NOT EVOLVED. The old labs engineered life. Lindqvist thinks it evolved: that is his error, the
  mirror of Hollweg's (who thinks his machines can reason).
- THE BROOD IS NOT A PERSON. No creature gets a reason, a want, a plan or a feeling. People may call a Brood Mother
  "Linnea". The creature never has a motive. NO BROOD HUB AND NO BROOD WAR MAP, EVER. The Naturalist (Valadares, and
  Lindqvist until he is taken) always tells the Brood's story.
- LOCAL PEOPLE ARE MINOR. The kings and captains of Kongo, Ethiopia, Goa, Manila and Nagasaki get a scene and a few
  lines. The story is carried by the cast we have, and each theatre makes one of them deeper.
- THE THREE RULES for every mission: SAY IT PLAINLY, NO WAITING (no idle stretch over about 45 seconds), CHECK THE FEEL.
- THE ROSTER LADDER: each mission brings one or two new pieces. Act 3 finishes the ladders (C12, K9, B9). From the
  third crossing on, every mission has the whole faction.
- THE SEA: no naval play. Voyages and sea battles live in still paintings. Captain Rui Amaral (proposed) and his ship,
  the Nossa Senhora da Boa Viagem, carry the story across the oceans.
- THE BALLOONIST: Father Inácio Lourenço (proposed) joined the army in d10 (flag `inacio` joined). Act 3 gives him his
  own flight.
- THE WORLDWIDE PLAN (owner answers of 2026-11-22): the climate rule (the Brood cannot live in the cold, the Clockwork
  rusts in the wet), local Catholic allies on every continent, Antarctica as a short mission (the Cold Forge), the third
  crossing as three missions on one night in any order, the finale in ROME.
- The campaign's structure: one story spine, both sides playable, choices carried forward, branches split and rejoin,
  REAL SPLITS ONLY AT THE ENDINGS. Two endings: ROME HOLDS, or ROME FALLS AND THE FAITH CARRIES ON, plus minor
  variations. The finale is in the PRESENT, where the heir puts the King's lessons into practice.

## Act 3 in plain words

The dangers leave Brazil by the Crown's own sea roads. A ship carries eggs to Africa. A ship carries "mining machines"
to the Indies. Act 3 is a tour of the world on fire, from 2781 to 2790.

On the war map the world opens. There are four THEATRES: THE AMERICAS, AFRICA, THE INDIES and THE SOUTHERN SEAS. Each
theatre is a short line of missions. The player picks which theatre to fight in next. A CLOCK turns the years. A
theatre left alone too long loses a colony. No player can save them all. What the player holds is the LEDGER that the
finale reads.

The three lines go on through the theatres:

- THE CROWN: Tomasz, a bishop now, goes where the faithful are in danger (Kongo, the Southern Seas). General Ruggieri
  brings Brazil's colonists home. Old Fairleigh rides out one last time in Virginia.
- THE CLOCKMAKER: Hollweg turns against what he made. He goes east with Father Ferrer after the Machine's seed. In
  Nagasaki the Christian clockmakers teach him how to STOP it. He ends the act in the King's palace, where c01 found him
  telling his story.
- THE NATURALIST: Valadares hunts the swarm up the Congo. He meets Lindqvist at a new pen in the forest, next to an old
  ruin of the labs. Lindqvist is TAKEN, which is how he comes before the King. In the ruin is the proof that the Brood
  was made.

In 2790, on the vigil of Pentecost, the Machine and the Brood turn on the Crown everywhere at once, and on each other.
THE NIGHT THE WORLD CAUGHT FIRE: three missions on three continents on one night, played in any order. Father Ferrer
dies that night at Goa, beside the man he warned in 2767.

Then the story reaches the present. In 2795 the King has told it all. The Machine comes south over the Alps from
Franconia (red since c01: the first Machine, come home to Rome). The Brood comes up from the Pontine Marshes, hatched
from eggs landed at the coast. The listener steps out of the shadow: THE HEIR. He takes command. On the third day of
the siege he makes THE FINAL CHOICE: HOLD ROME, or SAVE THE FLOCK and carry the faith to sea.

### THE CLOCK (the years turn)

- The war map shows the year, from 2781 to 2789.
- THE YEAR TURNS after every two Act 3 missions won (core or optional).
- When the year turns, the theatre NEGLECTED LONGEST loses one colony. (Ties: the hotter theatre loses: the swarm is
  quicker than the Machine.) The colony that falls is the one the climate favours: a wet, hot colony goes green (the
  Brood); a mining colony goes red (the Machine).
- A theatre whose core missions are all won is SETTLED. The clock takes nothing more from it. Its fate is in its flags.
- So the player's order matters. Rushing one theatre to its end leaves the others to the clock. Spreading out keeps
  every theatre alive but settles none early.
- Over the whole act about six to eight colonies fall on the clock. Optional missions are extra years of help (they
  hold a theatre) but they also turn the clock. Numbers are proposals: a small sim checks them (the build order).
- A fallen colony is not a defeat screen. It turns green or red on the map. Its people come home as refugees (a ledger
  line). It can return as a RECLAIM PART in a later mission in its theatre.

- ROME HOLDS is reached ONLY by choosing HOLD ROME and winning.
- ROME FALLS, THE FAITH CARRIES ON is reached by choosing SAVE THE FLOCK, or by choosing HOLD and losing.
- Losing HOLD is not a "game over". It plays out: the heir is carried wounded to the last boat (the hard way).
- Neither ending is "bad". The second is a defeat of the city and a victory of the faith. This is the campaign's
  frame from the start: the Crown barely holds; the Church survives.
